# Gaps

These are things a website will be asked for, which the four chats do not settle. Do not fill them from memory or from a similar event.

## The organisation

- No community interest company has been incorporated in these chats. No company number, registered office, director list, or constitution.
- No membership product, fee, or member benefits.
- No safeguarding policy, and no decision on juniors. The league minimum age of 15 was discussed. The Forestry form asked about under-18s. A draft entry list removed juniors and then put them back as a guess. Nothing was agreed.
- No public team page was agreed. Roles in the planning chat are working roles.
- The Facebook group exists and is used for events. Dan’s confirmed public channels are the Instagram account and the Gmail address. Whether the Facebook group is linked from the site was not decided.
- A domain was discussed, including onewheelscotland.org at a low registry price, with the race as a subdomain. Nothing in the chats says a domain was bought.
- The planning Drive folder and Trello board are private working tools.

## The race

- The 2027 date is not locked. The weekend they have asked about is 30 April to 2 May 2027, the May bank holiday. Forestry and Land Scotland both warned against bank holidays and appeared to accept that weekend. They still have to clarify it. Mollie prefers mid-April to mid-May for weather. June was also in the conversation because other events might move.
- Monday of that weekend is not an official event day. Litter-picking, a zip line, and a social ride were suggestions.
- They intend to run at least every other year, so the organisers do not burn out. After one conversation they joked about every year. Every other year is the stated intention, not a published calendar.
- The format is not locked. Practice day plus a race day is the working shape. On race day, Nico wants a mix of open practice, two attempts, and one-shot trails. Mollie likes one attempt after a full practice day. Dan likes one attempt on more trails. A handicap idea was vetoed. Nothing has been announced.
- The trail list is a shortlist, not a course. Twitcher may be closed for work at the top. Spooky Wood was discussed as famous and possibly unofficial.
- No ticket price. Jess said she cannot cost it until the venue, insurance, medical cover, food, and timing are priced. A rough break-even figure produced by a chatbot was rejected as too low. Other league events are often spoken of as £85 and up. A small “chill” ticket of £5–£10 was floated. None of these are prices.
- No cap has been signed. About 50 racers was the working size for a first event. Spectator numbers typed into a form were guesses.
- No decision on whether organisers ride for free, buy tickets, or are paid.
- The event logo is not final. The organisation logo is the supplied file.
- Sponsors were a list to approach only after Forestry, the bank-holiday question, and insurance are settled. Nobody on that list has agreed. Fungineers’ rail graphic needs permission before it is used.

## Permission and insurance

- National access for members of a governing body is a commitment to work on a framework, from the 14 October 2026 debrief. It is not a permit.
- The Forestry fee category is not confirmed. Figures spoken in the chat include a day rate around £115, a per-rider extra, a Glentress surcharge around 25 percent, parking around £6 each time a vehicle passes the barrier, a coffee-wagon figure around £130, and a new-event feasibility fee that appeared only in a question list. Treat every one of those as unconfirmed.
- The insurance limit, the insurer, and whether riders are covered are unknown. A £10 million figure came from a chatbot reading of Forestry paperwork, not from the phone call and not from a quote. A £20,000 anecdote about another forestry event is second-hand. A budget spreadsheet used £100 for insurance and £150 for first aid as placeholders. The organisers know those are not real.
- NFU Mutual does not cover this. A long broker list from BIBA was mostly unwilling to quote until 30 to 60 days before the event. Dan has used Brown & Brown in the UK, formerly Castle Insurance. A Hiscox contact came from an earlier Send it Surrey. No policy exists in the chats.
- They have not decided whether to insure spectators only and waiver the riders, or to insure participants. Later notes in the chat said a UK waiver cannot sign away injury caused by negligence, and the Forestry form asks for participants to be covered.
- It is unknown whether Forestry will ask for a motorsport governing body such as Motorsport UK or SACU.
- The café has not agreed catering. The campsite manager has not agreed the field. Go Ape, the uplift, and the bike shop have not been told. Two hotels were described as content with a few riders and not with charging. None of that is a booking.
- The Forestry contact’s name is not in the text export. Images were omitted. A separate demo enquiry was under a name that the chat rendered unclearly. Do not invent either name.
- Section 11, the order that suspends access rights on named trails, has not been applied for.

## Riding on the public site

- The chats treat riding a onewheel on a UK road or pavement as illegal, and also as something people do carefully on bike paths. The site should not publish routes, meeting points, or techniques for road riding.
- Which forests are happy to host a casual group, as opposed to which forests the group already visits, is not a legal opinion the chats can support. Beecraigs is fine on the trails and awkward at the café. Glentress staff have been friendly on casual days. That is experience, not permission for an event.
- Riders Retreat at Glentress used to feed and charge the group. The relationship ended. Do not send people there and do not tell the story.

## Kit pages

- The board-building chat is full of pack layouts, cell types, and voltages. None of that is a public specification. A kit page can say that people in the community build and maintain boards, and that experienced riders will help. It should not list a recommended voltage or a cell.
- There is no club board, no hire fleet, and no agreed beginner package.
- PPE is habitual, not a written club rule, except where the league requires a helmet check at a sanctioned race.
