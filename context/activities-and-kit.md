# Activities and kit

## What the club actually does

- Group rides, from a lunch-hour loop in Edinburgh to a full-day trail centre. The rides file and the Edinburgh file are mostly this.
- First goes for new riders. The chats repeatedly slow a day down, lend a board, and talk someone through a blue run. A Dundee cycle-path ride and a Portobello beach lesson are the gentle versions. Glentress taster trails are the trail version. (Edinburgh; Group rides)
- Demos where another site wants them. Glencoe in summer 2025 worked because mountain bikers were already there. Fort William and The Lecht have been suggested since. The race plan includes a supervised demo for people passing the event, and a later demo at a large event Forestry and Land Scotland mentioned for 2027. (Group rides; EOL plan)
- Trail maintenance with the Tweed Valley visitor team. See venues-and-insurance.md.
- Board building and repair. The VESC chat exists so this talk has a home. People print footpads, seal boards for rain, change tyres for each other, and talk newcomers through whether to change a controller before a battery. (Board building; Edinburgh)
- Racing in other people’s events, and now trying to host one. League rounds, Float the Highlands, Shred the Shires, Send it Surrey, race camps in the Algarve. (all four chats)

Snowboarding sits next to this. Several riders snowboard, James works at a dry slope, and people describe Glentress as the same feeling. A club page can mention that crossover. It is not a snowboard club. (Edinburgh; Group rides)

## The race, as a programme that is still soft

The draft Jess wrote on 7 July 2026 had an arrival day for registration, a practice day with every segment open and times visible, and a race day with one run at each trail, an endurance or team race, and a prize giving. By mid-October the one-shot rule was the argument, not the decision. See gaps.md.

Ideas that were liked and not adopted: a bingo card in a welcome pack, a hunt around Peebles shops for people wearing the event wristband, fire pits, a collaborative playlist, print-on-demand shirts so nothing is left in a box, flat Scottish trophies Mollie would make, crew shirts for volunteers. Trick competitions were played down because parents have caused scenes at them elsewhere. A pro and amateur split was rejected. One open field, with the league free to mark divisions afterwards if it wants to. (EOL plan)

Charging is part of the programme. Boards do not finish a day at Glentress on one charge if the riding is hard. The endurance-race talk at other events already includes sockets for the teams. (EOL plan; Group rides)

## Kit, at the level a public page can use

Riders in the community are on the whole range of boards: stock Pints and XRs, GTs, and VESC builds sold as complete boards or assembled at home. Fungineers and Floatwheel are the names that come up when someone asks what to buy. Home-built packs are common among the people who like the work. (Board building; Edinburgh, Oct 2025)

Scottish weather is the design constraint they repeat. Rain gets into footpads, so people seal grip tape and waterproof power trains. Wind stops a ride sooner than rain. Mud needs less speed. Ice wants a treaded tyre. A board that lives in a flat needs a bag or a tub so it can dry without covering the hall. (Edinburgh; Board building; Group rides)

Range is not one number. In the building chat, the same rider will quote a long easy day and a much shorter trail day on the same pack, and will tell a new owner that the percentage in an app is a poor guide. A public sentence is enough: trail days are planned around a charge at lunch, and a scenic day names the socket before the start. Do not publish cell counts, series counts, or voltages.

The kit people actually turn up in, once they are past the first car park:

- A helmet on trails and on open paths. Full-face is normal for racing and for steep trails. A skate helmet is what some city riders prefer because a full-face changes how pedestrians react. Mollie’s objection, in the same conversation, was that a cap does not protect a head. For anything the organisation hosts, the helmet is the rule they already use at league events. (Edinburgh, Mar 2026; Group rides, Mar 2026)
- Wrist guards. Jess said European events require a helmet and wrist guards as the minimum. A crash photo in the Edinburgh chat was the wrist guard doing its job. (Edinburgh, Jun 2025)
- Knee and shin guards for the people who race. Leatt guards are the set Jess, Dan, Mollie, and Nico use and recommend. They are a preference, not a club uniform. (Group rides, Nov 2025)
- Dan’s order of what to buy first, when someone asked: helmet, then wrists, then knees, then spine, then elbows, then shoulders. (Edinburgh, Oct 2025)

Shoes have to trigger the footpad. A police officer’s shoes did not, which is a useful demo detail: the board only drives when the rider is on it. (Edinburgh, Mar 2025)

## What the building chat must not become on the site

The chat is a workshop. It includes welders, cell choices, high-voltage packs, and step-by-step installs. High voltage is treated as something that can go badly wrong if someone is tired or rushing. That is the safety fact worth keeping: people who build packs do it slowly, on a clear bench, and they expect a new builder to use a proven kit instead. The procedures, the cell names, and the voltages stay in the chat.

A true public line is that the community includes people who build and maintain their own boards, that experienced riders will help a new owner choose and set a board up, and that a first VESC is usually a complete kit rather than a donor board and a soldering project. Nico and Dan both told a Pint owner that in March 2025. (Board building)

## Looking after the board in the group

People lend sensors, tyres, and tools, and they meet in car parks to hand parts over. Nico prints footpads for other riders. Andy has offered to help with controller installs. That mutual aid is the culture. It is not a shop, and the site should not look like one. (Board building; Group rides)
