# Rides and places

Group rides are how the community actually works. They are arranged in the chats a few days ahead, often the day before, and they move with the weather. A trail day is usually a car-park meeting, a morning session, food and a charge, then another session. A path day is a named start, a cycle path or coast, and a café or a shop that has already allowed a board to charge. Beginners and stock boards are planned for: greens, blues, and skills loops, with faster riders waiting. (Edinburgh; Group rides)

Weekends are when most people can leave the city. Edinburgh riders also do lunch hours and evenings on local paths. Winter riding happens, on treaded tyres when it is icy, and it is shorter. Nico’s standing line in August 2024 was that dry weekends would get rare, so ride them. (Edinburgh)

People post the plan in the Scotland rides chat and, when they remember, on the Facebook group. WhatsApp events were added because some riders are not on Facebook. (Group rides, Mar 2025)

## How a ride is hosted

- Say who is coming, what boards they are on, and whether the day will be technical or a cruise. The Glentress note on 28 February 2025 is the pattern: 10:30 in the car park, ride until 12:30, food and a charge, then another loop. Stock XRs and a first trail day were called out so people would not expect a race. (Edinburgh; Group rides)
- Book or warn the café if a group is going to want lunch and sockets. Andy and Nico already knew a Dundee Domino’s would charge boards. The V&A in Dundee has EV points if someone brings an adapter. (Group rides, Mar 2025)
- Leave the board where a car cannot run it over. That was the reminder at the first Edinburgh ride in this export, at Buzzard’s Nest, where buying lunch covered the car park. (Edinburgh, 11 Aug 2024)
- Slow down around people, dogs, and horses. On a shared path the person on the board is treated, inside the group, as the one who has to avoid everyone else. (Edinburgh, Jul 2025)
- Do not publish a minute-by-minute public route for an informal ride. In August 2026 a full plan for another UK event was handed to the police, who then knew every stop. (Group rides, 23 Aug 2026)

Riding on roads and pavements comes up constantly, because that is how people get between paths. Riders in the chats describe it as illegal in the UK, and they also describe years of careful path riding in Edinburgh with little trouble, including a police officer who asked for a go on a bike path. The public site should talk about trails, cycle paths, and permitted sites. It should not give a road-riding guide. (Edinburgh, Aug 2024, Nov 2024, Mar 2025, Mar 2026)

## Places they actually use

### Tweed Valley

Glentress is the shared trail centre. It is about an hour from Edinburgh and a long day from Aberdeen, so Aberdeen riders often miss it. Casual visits are common. Nico counted on the order of fourteen visits in 2025. Staff at the cafés have been friendly, and charging has been done in the café, in the toilets, at external sockets by the upper car park, and at the riders’ lounge. John took his children in October 2025 and was pointed at the taster trails, Apple Peel, and Admiral before the faster blues. (Edinburgh, Oct 2025; Group rides)

Trails named by people who ride there, as a menu rather than a course: Admiral, Magic Mushroom, Blue Velvet, Berm Baby Berm, Electric Blue, Turn and Burn, Twitcher, Smells Like Tweed Spirit, the taster and skills area. Spooky Wood is talked about as famous and easy to miss. Innerleithen and the Golfie are the next hill over. Nico has used the valley bike paths, including a check of the route toward a Sunday lunch stop in the town. A group ride into Peebles on roads was discussed later as unofficial. (Edinburgh; EOL plan)

Beecraigs, west of Edinburgh, is a regular trail day. Riders report no bother from bikers or walkers. Charging in the café is the problem: a council member of staff objects. The advice in the chat is to park near the skills area and stay out of the café with the boards. Canada Wood is the other regular, slightly closer for some of the west-of-Edinburgh riders, and it gets muddy. (Edinburgh, Feb 2026; Group rides)

Riders Retreat, the Glentress accommodation and food stop, fed and charged the group for about two years. By the planning period the relationship had broken down. The site should not recommend it.

### Edinburgh and the coast

Local riding is the Water of Leith, Leith and Granton, the Wardie Bay path, Cramond, Portobello beach at low tide, and the cycle network. Portobello sessions are planned around the tide and used for first lessons. Holyrood and the centre are ridden early, because the Festival and the crowds make them a bad idea later in the day. Dalkeith, the Pentlands, Bilston Glen, and Prestonpans appear as winter path days. Potholes and tree roots through cycle paths are the standing complaint. (Edinburgh)

John Muir Way has been suggested for a coastal winter ride. The Fife side, Inverkeithing to Burntisland, is described as an easy cycle-path day, with the path getting harder after Burntisland. Aberdour and Braefoot have been scouted: coastal path plus rough bike trails and jumps, not waymarked. (Edinburgh; Group rides, Apr 2026)

### Central Scotland

Carron Valley is a repeated trail day. It has dirt mountain-bike paths and gravel access roads. The car park Nico and Jess have used charges for parking and has toilets, and they did not find an outdoor socket. Stirling is the fallback charge, jokingly Tim Hortons. Callander and the south side of Loch Venachar have been ridden as a half-day. Comrie Croft was the August 2024 group ride, with a café emailed in advance and a Facebook event. Devilla Forest, Culross, and Preston Island are Fife and Forth stops. Bo’ness shore to Callendar Park is a mixed path-and-road loop Nico uses. Cathkin Braes is good after a dry week and a mud bath otherwise. Gravelfoyle at Aberfoyle is on the list and has been ridden by Glasgow members. Pollok and Chatelherault are Glasgow days. Dean Castle is a Kilmarnock ride. (Edinburgh; Group rides)

### North and west

Andy keeps a Google map of Aberdeenshire routes with parking and charging notes. The rides he has actually hosted or described: Deeside Way from Aberdeen toward Banchory and on to Ballater, with a long gap and no charge between Aboyne and Banchory; an Aberdeen city loop on trails and tarmac; Stonehaven to Dunnottar, which he will not run on a windy day; Newburgh to Balmedie on the beach, tide-dependent. A Monifieth to Dundee V&A cycle-path ride, with a midday meet, ran on 8 March 2025 and is the model for a family-pace day. (Group rides, Feb–Mar 2025)

Glencoe is a destination, not a weekly club night. Andy has scouted Inchtree Falls and the new cycle route toward the meeting of the three waters. Float the Highlands is based on that mountain. The Lecht was proposed in July 2026 as a demo day on refurbished bike trails, not yet a date. Fort William’s bike park was a demo idea after a rider who works there tried a board in June 2025. Laggan, Forest of Ae, and Tarland are trail centres people in the chat have been to or have firmly planned. Ae, ridden in March 2026, was recommended for mixed ability: flowy climbs, berms, and a steeper red with no compulsory gaps. (Group rides)

James Pitcher’s snowsports centre at Newmilns in Ayrshire has a disused bike loop and a large car park. In March 2025 he suggested a demo weekend for the centre’s 50th birthday. It stayed an idea. (Group rides)

## Demos and other people’s events

Demos are something the group already understands. A Glencoe session in summer 2025, run through Float the Highlands, was described afterwards as popular with mountain bikers, who pick the boards up quickly. Andy was asked for similar days elsewhere and said he can lend tents and features if someone else moves them. Jess and Nico wanted a try-out stand at a Glentress bike event for the same reason: a relationship, and a supervised first go. (Group rides, Nov 2025; EOL plan)

The same people travel to European Onewheel League rounds, Float the Highlands, Shred the Shires, Send it Surrey, and Onewheel Algarve race camps. Those are the scene around the club. They are not Onewheel Scotland events.
