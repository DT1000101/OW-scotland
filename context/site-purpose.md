# What the site is for

Onewheel Scotland is the public name of a rider group that already meets, rides, and builds boards across Scotland. The organisation site is the page that should come up when a landowner, an insurer, a venue, or a new rider looks the group up.

The planning chat that runs from 6 July 2026 to 18 October 2026 is where they said this out loud. They are trying to put on a first Scottish round of the European Onewheel League at Glentress, called Tweed Valley Senduro, presented by Onewheel Scotland. Forestry and Land Scotland class onewheels as motorised vehicles. A small group of riders with a WhatsApp chat does not look like a body that can book a forest, close trails, or buy insurance. The site is how they want to look: named, calm, and already doing the ordinary work of a club.

Sources below are the four exports. “EOL plan” is the chat titled The degenerates (EOL plan chat). “Edinburgh” is Onewheel Edinburgh. “Group rides” is Onewheel Scotland Group Rides. “Board building” is the VESC chat. “Dan” is brand he confirmed outside the chats.

## Who it is for

- Forestry and Land Scotland, and Scottish Borders Council, who would have to permit a trail closure. (EOL plan, 14 Oct 2026 debrief)
- Insurers and brokers, who have already turned the group away or said they will only quote close to the date. (EOL plan)
- The Glentress campsite, the café, and other people on the hill who need a short account of what is being asked. (EOL plan)
- Local businesses in Peebles and the Tweed Valley, later, for practical help rather than cash. (EOL plan)
- Riders who already use the WhatsApp community, and people who find the group from a ride or a demo. (Edinburgh, Group rides)
- The European Onewheel League, which needs an organiser application and a public calendar entry. (EOL plan, 14–18 Oct 2026)

## What it has to do

- Give the organisation a public name, a contact, and a plain description of the riding.
- Sit above the event. Someone who searches the organisation should see the overview, then Tweed Valley Senduro. The event already has its own site. (EOL plan; Dan has a draft at tweed-valley-senduro.vercel.app)
- Describe a race in the words they want a risk assessment to use: a small field, one rider at a time, existing one-way mountain-bike trails, ridden in the direction they were built, helmets and wrist guards, a briefing, first aid, marshals. (EOL plan)
- Show the club activity that already happens without a permit: group rides, help for new riders, trail-care days with Forestry and Land Scotland visitor services, and demos where another site has asked for them. (Edinburgh, Group rides, EOL plan)
- Stay useful if the 2027 date slips. The organisation is the lasting thing. The race is one project. (EOL plan)

## What they are asking the land for

On 14 October 2026 Jess and Mollie spoke to Forestry and Land Scotland. Their debrief, not a transcript of the call, says:

- Onewheels are priced and treated like a cycle event, and classed as motorised.
- The contact would work toward a national arrangement so members of a governing body, Onewheel Scotland or the European Onewheel League, could use Forestry and Land Scotland sites.
- That arrangement is a piece of work they were promised, not a permission already granted.
- A specific event still needs a risk assessment, a trail plan, and a Section 11 order from Scottish Borders Council if trails are closed to other users.

The site should be able to carry that story without claiming the permission exists.

## What it should not be

Tweed Valley Senduro is a race weekend organised by Onewheel Scotland riders. Float the Highlands and Shred the Shires are other UK events in the same sport. The public site can name them as neighbours. The arguments about dates and control stay off the site. (EOL plan)

Informal rides are not the race. Publishing a full route and timetable for an ordinary ride has already drawn police attention at another UK event, because the boards sit in a grey area on public roads and paths. (Group rides, 23 Aug 2026) The organisation site should describe social rides as social rides, and describe the race only as a permitted, insured, closed course once those things are true.
