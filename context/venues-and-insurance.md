# Venues and insurance

This is the account the organisation site has to be able to support. It comes from the planning chat’s debrief of a Forestry and Land Scotland call on 14 October 2026, plus earlier arguments in the rides chat about why an event cannot be informal. Figures are what people said in the chat. None of them are a quote the site can print as a price.

## The standard they set themselves

In February 2026, talking about Glentress as a place that could hold a big weekend, Andy’s line was that an organised race on public land without permission puts the organiser in serious legal trouble, and that “ask forgiveness” is not available once a hundred people are involved. Forestry and Land Scotland would have to say yes. The electric-vehicle question was named as the hard part. (Group rides, 15 Feb 2026)

By October the planning group had turned that into a description they were willing to send:

- A Scottish round of the European Onewheel League.
- Single-wheeled self-balancing electric boards.
- A closed course on established mountain-bike trails at Glentress, ridden one at a time, in the direction the trails were built.
- No new man-made features. Using the trails as designed is also how they hope to avoid being classed as course designers. That still has to be checked with Forestry.
- Helmets and wrist guards for anyone on a board, racers and non-racers.
- A briefing, declarations, marshals, first aid, and a written risk assessment.
- Riders yield to everyone else. Demos are supervised by experienced riders.

The risk assessment has to explain how a board stays under a rider, and what an expected and an unexpected dismount look like. Forestry’s worry, as relayed from the call, is a board travelling on without the rider and meeting the public. The group agreed to describe the footpad sensor and the fail-safes that cut the motor when the nose lifts or the rider comes off, and to keep the slang word for that failure out of the letter. They also agreed not to write that they have ridden the hill for years without incident, because that sentence admits use without permission. (EOL plan, 14–16 Oct 2026)

European Onewheel League events were, by March 2026, checking helmets at the start. Andy’s view in the rides chat was that sanctioned events have to be stricter, and that medical cover is the event’s job and depends on the course and the distance to a hospital. A start referee checks kit. An approved helmet can then carry a league sticker. (Group rides, 5 Mar 2026)

## What the 14 October call is said to have produced

Jess and Mollie made the call. The notes are their debrief.

- Onewheels are classed as motorised vehicles.
- The price discussed was in the cycle-event band. A day figure around £115 was mentioned.
- The Forestry contact, whose name is not in the text, would work on a national framework so that members of a governing body, Onewheel Scotland or the European Onewheel League, could use Forestry and Land Scotland land. Jess looked up a public figure that Forestry and Land Scotland holds about 8 percent of Scotland, about 6,400 square kilometres. That is her figure from the chat, not a number to put on a poster as if the group had been granted it.
- A Section 11 order from Scottish Borders Council is required to suspend outdoor access and close specific trails. Opening and closing in waves is easier than shutting the whole hill. Their suggestion was that half the trails stay open while half are closed.
- The overflow field at the bottom camp can be opened without a field hire if they give notice so the horses are taken off, with at least two weeks of the field being empty of horses. The field has no toilets or showers. The centre’s facilities are available in opening hours. Outside those hours the group would be on its own.
- The campsite manager is Blair, at Glentress Forest Lodges. The debrief says he is less keen than the previous manager. The ask is notice, not a favour he has agreed.
- Parking was described as £6 each time a vehicle comes in or out.
- The café has exclusive catering, or at least first refusal. A small coffee wagon was mentioned at about £130. Structures on site are probably not charged. Music brings Scottish Borders Council in, so skipping music makes the permission simpler.
- Twitcher may be closed while the top is reshaped. The other trails they named were not rejected on the call.
- Public liability insurance is required to use the venue.
- Forestry are introducing them to a large event in late June or early July 2027 for a supervised demo. A separate person had asked for a call about demos and leaflets. Both of those are introductions, not bookings.

A direct Glentress address appeared in an email signature. The name did not survive into the text export.

## Money that is not agreed

Jess’s back-of-envelope for a bare venue bill, up to 50 people, was on the order of £240, built from the £115, a small per-person charge, and another fixed line. Mollie later read a rate card as £115 a day including 50 participants, about £1.70 for each person after that, plus a Glentress surcharge around 25 percent, with stalls and camping extra. A £400-a-day new-event feasibility fee was in a list of questions Dan drafted. It was not confirmed on the call. Until Forestry confirm which category the boards fall into, none of this is a budget.

Insurance is the open bill. NFU Mutual does not cover it and pointed them at BIBA. The broker list is long, and many will not quote until 30 to 60 days before the event. The group can see two shapes of policy: cover for the public and spectators, or cover that includes the riders. They leaned toward public cover plus waivers, and then ran into the opposite problem: a chatbot reading of the Forestry event agreement talked about a high limit and about participants being covered, and someone in the chat noted that a UK waiver cannot exclude death or injury caused by negligence. The £10 million figure belongs to that reading. It was not said on the phone call. An anecdote that another forestry site had been quoted about £20,000 is second-hand, via Kate, and unverified. The planning spreadsheet carried £100 for insurance and £150 for first aid so the other cells would add up. Everyone in the chat treated those as fiction.

Dan’s own broker is Brown & Brown in the UK, previously Castle Insurance. A Hiscox contact had come from the first Send it Surrey, through Chan. Neither has produced a policy in these chats.

## Trail care

Forestry and Land Scotland visitor services at Tweed Valley run volunteer trail days, roughly every four weeks, booked through Eventbrite:

https://www.eventbrite.co.uk/o/forestry-and-land-scotland-visitor-services-tweed-valley-34105789963

A dig day on 7 November 2026 was in the chat. Jess could go. Mollie was working. The point they agreed is relationship, not a favour to trade for a permit. The public site can say the club turns up to look after trails. It should not say the dig days have bought the race.

## Camping, food, and the town

For the race weekend, the working hospitality picture is: the café first, and outside vendors only if the café declines. A pizza truck from Bo’ness was mentioned. Haggis-topped chips was the Scottish food joke that they were willing to mean. Charging for boards has to be planned, because a trail day without a socket ends early. Two hotels in the valley were said to be fine with a handful of riders and not fine with a bank of chargers. Go Ape, the uplift operator, and the bike shop were on a list of neighbours to tell, not people who had been told.

Fire pits, a shared playlist, and a live drone were all discussed and then hedged. Music licensing and Borders Council made music look expensive. Still photographs were preferred to a live drone. None of this is a programme.

## How the page should read to an insurer

Short, specific, and smaller than the anxiety in the chat. A first event on the order of 50 racers, one at a time, on trails that already run one way, with kit rules, a briefing, and medical cover appropriate to the hill. The organisation can say it is in the process of becoming a community interest company. It cannot say it is one. It can say it has asked Forestry and Land Scotland for a proper permission. It cannot say it has one.
